title: "Accounts and wallets"
description: "Understand how one Aomi account can link multiple wallets, and how each wallet keeps its own signing policy and revocable permissions."
---

Your Aomi account is your identity across the product. It keeps your threads,
Apps, settings, plan, and usage together. Wallets sit beneath that account as
separate signing endpoints.

This separation matters because one person may use an email login, a browser
wallet, an embedded wallet, and a Solana wallet at the same time. A wallet
address identifies a key on a chain. It does not identify the whole person.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Aomi account<br/>threads, Apps, settings, and usage"]
    I["Sign-in methods<br/>email, social, or wallet"]
    E["EVM wallet<br/>0x12…89 · Ask me"]
    S["Solana wallet<br/>7gK…4pQ · Locked"]
    W["Embedded wallet<br/>0xA4…7C · Automatic signing"]
    G["Revocable delegated grant<br/>covers this wallet"]

    I --> A
    A --> E
    A --> S
    A --> W
    G --> W
```

The account is the common owner. Each wallet remains independent: it has its
own address, chain family, provider, signing mode, and authorization history.
Changing one wallet does not silently change another.

## What belongs to the account

Settings separates account access from signing permission.

| Tab | What you manage |
| --- | --- |
| **General** | Appearance, defaults, account overview, plan, and monthly allowance |
| **Account** | Your profile, **Wallets & access**, social sign-in methods, and the session on this device |
| **Safety** | Transaction safety, per-wallet **Signing**, and provider-delegated **Automatic signing** |
| **Usage** | Account-wide spend, allowance, and statements |

## Connected, linked, and Active

These describe different relationships:

- **Connected** means a wallet is available to this browser through a wallet
  app or provider. A connection alone does not prove ownership to Aomi.
- **Linked** means Aomi has verified the wallet's relationship to your account.
  A linked wallet can remain on the account while disconnected from this device.
- **Active** identifies the usable wallet selected for signing in its chain
  family on this device. Selecting it does not enable automatic signing.

You can use different active wallets for EVM and Solana. If a selected wallet
needs reconnection or another verification step, complete that step before it
can sign. A saved selection alone is not signing authority.

Disconnecting a wallet ends its local connection. Removing it from the account
removes its account relationship. Neither action moves its funds or deletes
its keys.

## How wallets become linked

Aomi accepts two kinds of proof:

- **Self-custody proof.** Your wallet signs a short-lived message. Aomi verifies
  the signer before linking the address. Typing or pasting an address is never
  enough to claim it.
- **Provider proof.** An embedded-wallet provider identifies the wallet created
  for your authenticated login. Aomi records the provider relationship and
  links the public address to your account.

Each public address belongs to one account. On EVM networks, the same address
represents the same key across EVM chains, so it carries one signing policy.
Solana keys are recorded separately.

<Note>
Linking proves ownership or provider provenance. It does not give an App
permission to sign. Signing authority is configured separately for each
wallet.
</Note>

## Every wallet has its own signing policy

In **Settings → Safety → Signing**, each user-controlled wallet has three
aligned choices. A choice that the wallet cannot support stays disabled.

| UI choice | Stored mode | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ask me** | `manual` | You inspect and approve each transaction in your wallet. |
| **Auto** | `client_auto` | A compatible Privy or Para wallet client signs without a separate prompt for each transaction. It still needs an available signing client. |
| **Locked** | `denied` | The wallet cannot sign until you change its policy. |

External browser wallets currently offer **Ask me** and **Locked**; their
**Auto** choice is disabled. Provider-managed agent wallets have separate
controls under **Automatic signing** because you do not hold their key.

## Automatic signing has two gates

The **Automatic signing** section configures provider-delegated signing for
work that can continue when this browser is closed. It uses the stored mode
`auto`, which is different from the **Auto** choice in the Signing row.

Two independent records must agree:

1. This wallet's policy must permit provider-delegated automatic signing.
2. A live delegated grant must cover that exact wallet.

Logging in through Privy or Para does not grant this permission. A delegation
also cannot override a wallet that is **Ask me** or **Locked**.

Privy uses a separate one-time delegation for an eligible wallet. Para uses a
separate provider-managed agent wallet; creating it does not change the
user-controlled login wallet. Read [Configure approvals](/security/configure-approvals)
for setup and revocation.

<Warning>
Automatic signing removes the per-transaction prompt. Enable it only for a
wallet whose intended use and risk you understand. An expired or revoked grant
blocks signing even if the saved policy still permits it.
</Warning>

## Policy changes require a signature

Aomi does not change a wallet policy from an authenticated click alone. The
change includes the account, wallet, requested mode, expiry, and current policy
version, then requires a qualifying linked-wallet signature. Loosening a policy
requires that wallet to sign; a qualifying wallet on the account can authorize
a tighter policy.

This protects three boundaries:

- A wallet cannot be attached to a different account without proof.
- A stale or replayed policy change cannot overwrite a newer decision.
- Granting more autonomy requires cryptographic approval, not just a logged-in
  browser session.

The Settings flow handles this ceremony for you. If a signature is rejected or
the policy changed in another session, nothing changes and you can try again
from the current state.

## When two sign-in methods open different accounts

A wallet or provider login may already belong to another Aomi account. Proving
that login while signed in can produce a **Merge accounts** offer. An address
or matching email shown in the interface is not enough: Aomi first verifies a
wallet signature or provider credential for the other account.

The offer shows the other account's chats, wallets, credits, and saved keys
that will not carry over. Confirming **Merge into this account** moves its
chats, wallets, and credits into the account you are signed in to. The other
account closes, and its linked sign-in methods resolve to the retained account.
Where offered, **Switch to that account instead** changes this browser's
session without merging the accounts.

<Warning>
Review the destination before merging. Saved model keys and App secrets from
the other account are dropped, including keys that do not conflict. Re-enter
them on the retained account if needed. Existing keys on the retained account
stay intact. The other account's signed-in browser and widget sessions end.
</Warning>

A merge combines account records. It does not move onchain funds, combine
private keys, or turn on automatic signing for all wallets. Each wallet keeps
its signing policy and provider-delegation requirements.

## Account sessions and proof

An account session identifies the account making a request. It does not prove
control of a new wallet or authorize a policy change by itself. Wallet linking
and policy changes require their own qualifying proof.

A provider credential must be verified for its provider and application;
matching email text is not a substitute. An API token authorizes requests
within its permissions, rather than acting as a wallet signature. Keep tokens
private and revoke them when no longer needed.

## What Aomi stores

| Aomi stores | Aomi does not store |
| --- | --- |
| Your canonical account identifier | A private key for your self-custody wallet |
| Login-provider identities used to resolve that account | A seed phrase or wallet recovery secret |
| Linked public addresses and chain families | Permission inferred from an address alone |
| One signing policy and version per wallet | An unlimited right to sign because you logged in |
| References to active, expired, or revoked delegated grants | A delegated grant that bypasses the wallet policy |

Every signature is produced where the key lives: in your wallet, in your own
key-holding client, or through a supported provider under an active delegated
grant. Aomi verifies the signature and enforces the selected policy.

## Common account setups

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Email login with an embedded wallet">
    Your login resolves to one Aomi account. The provider-created wallet appears
    beneath it. It can remain **Ask me**, use **Auto** with an available
    compatible client, or use provider-delegated **Automatic signing** when a
    live grant supports that exact wallet.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="One browser wallet on several EVM networks">
    The address represents one EVM key, so Aomi links it once and applies one
    signing policy across EVM chains. Each transaction still specifies its
    target network.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Several wallets on one account">
    Link each wallet with its own proof. Your history and usage stay together at
    the account level, while each wallet keeps an independent policy. You can
    leave a trading wallet **Ask me** and lock a second wallet.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A background action with an Ask me wallet">
    The action may continue preparing work, but the transaction cannot complete
    until you sign. Use **Auto** for an available compatible client, or configure
    **Automatic signing** for eligible provider-delegated background work.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
